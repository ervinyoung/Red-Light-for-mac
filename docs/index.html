Red Shift

Every blue subpixel, off. Slowly.

Night Shift warms. Red Light removes: at 60% the blue channel sits at zero at the display's own transfer table, and text stays crisp. It walks there across a thirty-minute fade from sunset, in steps too small to catch.

Room light

The darker the room, the redder the screen.

At night, Red Light reads your Mac's ambient light sensor. A lit room gets no red at all. As the lamps go off, Red Shift rises with them, easing in over half a minute. Nudge it on a few nights and it learns how you like your room.

Smart

It learns your night, then runs it for you.

Dusk to ease in, Night to settle, Movie to drop the whole screen back, Deep night for the last hour — each one moves the display, the keyboard and the screen shade together. Settle on the same thing three nights out of fourteen and it quietly becomes your default, sunset time included. It never fights a change in the moment. It just stops needing to be told.

Two weeks of it and read your own deep sleep and latency. n = 1, like everything that works.
