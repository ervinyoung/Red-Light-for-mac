What the glass does to you

There is a cell at the back of the eye that does not see. It has no part in seeing. It only tells the body what hour it is, and it answers to blue. It answered to the sky for as long as there have been eyes and now it answers to the glass, and the glass says noon at midnight, and the body believes it.

So the body holds the melatonin back. It waits for a dusk that does not come. And you lie there in the dark with the day still running in your blood.

The cell is a melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cell, most sensitive near 480 nm, squarely in the blue that screens emit. Evening exposure to that light measurably suppresses melatonin and delays the circadian clock. Warming the white point helps. Taking the blue out entirely helps more.

The problem with tints

A veil over the glass

Lay a red film across everything and everything becomes the same red. You cannot read a map in it. You cannot tell the link from the word beside it.

Apple's Color Tint filter maps every pixel to its brightness and mixes toward one hue. Nothing disappears, but hue collapses. Past 40% it is a blob.

The problem with warmth

A ceiling on warm

Night Shift turns the light gold and stops there, and the blue is still in it. It was only ever a warmer noon.

White-point shifting scales the display's color channels. It keeps text crisp but never drives blue to zero, and it has nowhere to go past its own ceiling.

What Nightfall does

Both, each where it is right

First the blue is unmade at the source, until there is none. Then, and only then, a little red is folded in so that what was green does not fall to black.

Channel scaling from 0 to 60% until blue is zero. Above 60%, a modest luminance-preserving tint recovers the green being removed. One slider.

Drag it and watch the blue leave

This is the same curve the app uses, applied to a mock window. Notice that the text stays readable the whole way, and that blue is gone at 60% before any tint arrives.

What it does when the sun goes down

Warmth

The blue, unmade

Not a film laid over the glass but the light itself, taken apart at the source. What is left is red and gold and you can still read by it.

Blue and green are scaled at the display's own transfer table, the layer beneath every app. Text stays crisp. Screenshots are untouched.

Keyboard

Dimmer than the machine allows

The keys go down to a tenth of the least the slider will give you. And after five seconds of stillness, dark.

The backlight keeps responding below the system slider's floor. Nightfall reaches 0.1%, and turns it off after inactivity, on a timer you choose.

Learning

It watches and says nothing

Turn it down one night and it lets you. Turn it down three nights and it knows, and tells you once, and does not ask again.

Consistent adjustments on three of the last fourteen nights become the default. Switch it on early three evenings and sunset moves. Never more than two hours. One notification, at most once a day. Undo with one command.

The ritual

It does not ask you to remember. It keeps the hour for you, the way the sky did.

I

Sunset

It knows where you are and when the sun goes down there. Over thirty minutes the blue drains out and the keys dim. You will not see it happen. You will only notice, later, that it has.

II

Night

Presets for the kind of night it is. Dusk for reading. Night for work. Deep for the last hour. Movie, with the screen shaded down below the hardware minimum.

III

Pause

Color work at eleven. A call. Pause it for an hour or until sunrise and it restores your day exactly, and learns nothing from what you do meanwhile.

IV

Sunrise

It gives the day back. Whatever you had before dusk, to the setting. Open the lid at any hour and it is already right.

Questions

Isn't this just Night Shift?

Night Shift warms the white point and stops. Blue is still in the light, a little less of it. Nightfall drives blue to zero at 60% and then keeps going, and it does the keyboard, the shade, the fade, the pause, and the learning. Night Shift is a warmer noon. This is dusk.

Will it make me sleep?

It removes a signal that tells your body to stay awake. That is all any light hygiene can do, and it is not nothing: evening blue light is one of the best-documented disruptors of melatonin and circadian timing. The rest, the caffeine and the hour and the phone on the nightstand, is yours.

Does it phone home?

No. There is no server. Location is read from the Mac through Location Services, used to compute sunrise and sunset, and written to a file in your home folder. Nothing leaves the machine. If you would rather, type coordinates in by hand and it will never ask.

What does it need?

An Apple silicon Mac on macOS 26 or later, and the Xcode Command Line Tools to build it. Clone the repository and run the installer. It builds the engine and the menu bar app from source, so you can read every line first.

Is it safe? Does it use private APIs?

The color work uses Apple's public MediaAccessibility and CoreGraphics frameworks. The keyboard control uses a private CoreBrightness interface, the same one the system's own slider uses. If Apple changes it, the keyboard part may stop working until it is updated. Everything else will carry on. The display's color tables are restored the moment the app quits, so nothing can get stuck.

I don't have an OLED. Does that matter?

No. Blue light is blue light on any panel. The red-blob problem people hit with heavy tints is the tint collapsing hue, not the display. Nightfall's curve is designed so that does not happen.

Nightfall is a tool for reducing evening blue light exposure. It is not a medical device and makes no medical claims. If you have a sleep disorder, talk to a clinician. Sources: Chang et al., PNAS 2015, on evening light-emitting devices and melatonin; Berson et al., Science 2002, on melanopsin ganglion cells.
